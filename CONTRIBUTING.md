# Contributing to Debaser's Open Policies

We're glad you're here.

Whether you're an arts organization looking to adapt these policies for your own use, or someone who spotted a typo or an improvement — welcome. This document walks you through everything you need to get started.

---

## Using These Policies for Your Organization

The most common reason to be here is to adapt these policies for your own organization. You don't need to know much about GitHub to do it — this guide walks you through every step.

### What is GitHub, and why does it matter here?

GitHub is a website for storing and tracking changes to text files. We use it because it gives us a permanent, public record of every version of our policies, and it makes it easy for anyone to copy them and make their own version.

When you "fork" this repository, you're making your own copy of all our files on GitHub — under your own account. You can then edit your copy as much as you like without affecting ours.

---

## How to Fork and Adapt These Policies

You'll need a free GitHub account. If you don't have one, go to [github.com](https://github.com) and click "Sign up" — it takes about two minutes.

### Step 1: Fork this repository

1. Make sure you're signed into GitHub
2. Go to this repository's page: https://github.com/brodieconley/debaser-policies
3. Click the **Fork** button near the top-right of the page
4. GitHub will ask you where to put the fork — choose your account
5. Click **Create fork**

You now have your own copy of all three policy documents, stored under your GitHub account.

### Step 2: Find your forked copy

After forking, GitHub will take you to your copy automatically. You'll see the same files, but the address at the top of the page will now say something like `your-username/debaser-policies` instead of `brodieconley/debaser-policies`.

### Step 3: Edit a policy file

1. Click on the `policies/` folder in the file list
2. Click on the file you want to edit (e.g., `mandate-and-vision.md`)
3. Click the **pencil icon** (Edit this file) near the top-right of the file view
4. Make your changes directly in the browser editor

> **Tip:** The files use Markdown formatting. Don't worry too much about the syntax — for most changes (replacing names, updating dates), you're just editing plain text.

### Step 4: Save your changes

When you're done editing:
1. Scroll down to the **"Commit changes"** box
2. Write a short description of what you changed (e.g., "Replace Debaser with [your org name]")
3. Click **Commit changes**

Repeat for each file you want to edit.

### Step 5: Download your adapted files

Once you've made your edits, you can download the files:
- Click on a file → click the **Raw** button → right-click and Save As
- Or, from the main page of your repository, click the green **Code** button and choose **Download ZIP**

## Adapter's Checklist

When adapting these policies for your organization, work through the following items. Some are quick find-and-replaces; others involve genuine decisions about your organization's values and structure.

### All Documents

- [ ] **Replace "Debaser" with your organization's name throughout all three documents.** Use your editor's Find & Replace (Ctrl+H or Cmd+H) to catch every instance.

- [ ] **Update all `Effective Date:` fields** to the date your board adopted the policy. These dates carry legal and governance weight — use the actual adoption date.

- [ ] **Adjust governance titles** — these policies reference "Board Chair" and "Vice-Chair." If your organization uses different titles (Executive Director, Steering Committee Co-Chair, etc.), update accordingly throughout. Especially check the Conflict of Interest Policy, which references these titles in the disclosure and resolution process.

- [ ] **Replace all Debaser contact information** (email: hello@debaser.ca, address: 2 Daly Ave) with your organization's contact details. Check the Conflict of Interest Policy appendices — they contain the address as a letterhead.

- [ ] **Check for Ontario/Canada-specific language** — references to provincial law, federal legislation, and the Ottawa region appear in the Conflict of Interest Policy. Review and adapt for your province, state, or country as appropriate.

### Conflict of Interest Policy

- [ ] **Review the definitions of "Members" and "participants"** — this policy defines Members as Board Directors, Officers, and Committee Members. If your organization uses different language for these roles, or if your governance structure is different (e.g., you have staff voting members, or a co-op model), review whether these definitions still apply.

- [ ] **Review the "Policy Inspiration" section** — we credit the organizations we learned from. You may want to update this section to credit Debaser (and any other sources you drew from) in the same spirit.

### Partnership Policy

- [ ] **PACBI clause (Section 3, item 3)** — This clause commits Debaser to upholding the principles of the Palestinian Campaign for the Academic and Cultural Boycott of Israel. This reflects Debaser's values and a position we stand behind. When adapting this policy, you'll need to decide whether this clause applies to your organization's values and context. You can:
  - **Keep it** — if your organization shares this position
  - **Remove it** — if it doesn't apply
  - **Replace it** — with your own ethical partnership criteria or a general values-alignment clause

  This is a meaningful decision; don't simply copy it without considering whether you mean it.

- [ ] **Review "For-profit partner" definitions** — our policy distinguishes between community partners and for-profit partners. Check whether this framing fits your partnerships landscape.

### Mandate and Vision

- [ ] **Rewrite the 8-year vision** — the vision section ("Our Vision for Debaser in 8 Years") is specific to Debaser's context, goals, and ambitions. This section is meant to be replaced entirely with your organization's own vision. Use our structure if helpful, but make the content yours.

- [ ] **Review the Mandate** — the mandate items (Catalyze, Support underrepresented artists, Create inclusive spaces) are also Debaser-specific. Adapt them to reflect your actual mandate and values.

## Contributing Improvements Back

If you've spotted an error, have a suggestion, or have made an improvement that could benefit others, we'd love to hear from you.

### What makes a good contribution

- **Corrections** — typos, broken links, outdated references, grammatical errors
- **Clarifications** — places where the policy language is ambiguous or harder to understand than it needs to be
- **Structural improvements** — suggestions to make the documents easier to navigate or use
- **Adaptation insights** — if you adapted these policies for a different jurisdiction or governance model and made changes that others would benefit from

We're less interested in:
- Changes that reflect your organization's specific context rather than a general improvement
- Rewrites of content that reflect Debaser's values (we stand behind the content as written)

### How to submit a pull request

A pull request is a way to propose changes to someone else's repository. Here's how:

1. **Fork and edit** — follow the steps above to fork and make your changes in your copy
2. **Go to the Pull Requests tab** on our repository (https://github.com/brodieconley/debaser-policies/pulls)
3. Click **New pull request**
4. Click **compare across forks**, then select your fork and branch
5. Write a clear description of what you changed and why
6. Click **Create pull request**

We'll review it and respond within a few weeks. We may ask questions, suggest changes, or merge it as-is.

### What to expect

- Debaser reviews all pull requests before merging — nothing goes in without our review
- We aim to respond to PRs within 2–4 weeks
- We may close PRs that don't fit the scope of this repository, but we'll tell you why

If you adapt these policies, we ask that you credit us. The [CC BY-SA 4.0 license](LICENSE) requires attribution — here's the format we prefer:

You can put this in a footer, a policy history section, or wherever it makes sense in your document.
